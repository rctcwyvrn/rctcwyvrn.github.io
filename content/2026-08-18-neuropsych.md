## Brain silly

Typically, autistic brains have very "spike-y" IQ profiles, where they typically have very pronounced strengths and weaknesses.

My brain is no exception

### IQ profile
<img src="/images/neuropsych-3.png" width="800"/>

Averaged out at 119 IQ, landing in the 90th percentile, though they did specifically say

> Lily’s Full Scale IQ must be interpreted with caution, however. There is a scaled score point difference of 27 between the Perceptual Reasoning and Working Memory Indexes. Such a difference is rare, and statistically significant, occurring in less than 3.5% of same age peers.

Which is to say, so spikey that its hard to really gauge where my IQ lands. Like my perceptual reasoning lands quite high at 127, while my working memory lands perfectly average at 100.

### IQ subtests

The subtests really show the story on my spikiness

<img src="/images/neuropsych-4.png" width="800"/>

Verbal comprehension was fairly spikey and processing speed and perceptual reasoning landed fairly flat.

However my working memory results are crazy.

> The Working Memory Index measured Lily’s ability to register, maintain, and manipulate visual and auditory information in conscious awareness, which requires attention and concentration, as well as visual and auditory discrimination.

> Lily’s performance here suggests a significant discrepancy in her working memory: while her auditory working memory was strongly underdeveloped, her ability to mentally manipulate information in her auditory working memory using numerical reasoning is exceptionally strong.

Whoops.

> This can mean that when working memory tasks are meaningful, structured, or involve problem-solving or reasoning, she can utilize strategies and perform extremely well. This pattern is often seen in individuals with ADHD, autism spectrum traits, or executive functioning weaknesses, where spontaneous short-term attention to unstructured auditory information is weak, but higher order working memory tied to logic or interest areas is much stronger.

This is honestly super interesting information, if a little silly.

My brain so silly. So good at structured tasks and so so so bad at unstructured memorization.

I'm so so bad at remembering phone numbers and other bits of like "incidental" information.

Tell me something and I will forget it instantly.

Drive me somewhere a couple times and I'll remember the layout of the parking lot for life.

## ADHD maybe???

So my ADHD results were _weird_.

On the one hand, one psychologist:
> Lily’s attention was very inconsistent.

On the other hand, the other psychologist:
> With regard to attention, Lily generally sustained her attention and concentration on tasks. There was no notable disturbance of activity levels, and no signs of hyperactivity.

On the CPT test:
> Interestingly, though, she was able to attend well to the repetitive continuous performance task on the CPT where no abnormal scores were noted

And on the screener:
> On the ADHD Symptom checklist, Lily just missed clinical criteria for ADHD of combined subtype.

Some more tests came back inconclusive

<img src="/images/neuropsych-11.png" width="800"/>

So in the end, I got an ADHD _secondary to mood_ diagnosis. That is to say, attention deficits but caused by mood disorders rather than by the typical ADHD dopamine mechanisms???

A weird diagnosis, and one that I want a second opinion on.
